## Problem description
Crop disease is usually noticed after it has spread, and smallholder farmers rarely have quick access to expert screening. Plant-disease image models are typically trained and tested on clean laboratory photos, where they look near-perfect, then degrade on real field images with messy backgrounds and uneven lighting. A useful tool must be tested under field conditions and be honest about its uncertainty.

## Proposed solution
CropVision AI classifies a tomato leaf photo into one of eight conditions, shows a confidence score and a Grad-CAM heatmap of the regions that drove the prediction, and flags low-confidence cases for expert review instead of forcing a label. Its core contribution is a **lab-to-field evaluation**: the model is trained on PlantVillage (lab) images and tested only on unseen PlantDoc (field) images, and we report the accuracy gap, whether the model's confidence can be trusted, and how much field data helps close the gap.

## Key features
- Eight tomato classes: bacterial spot, early blight, late blight, leaf mold, Septoria leaf spot, mosaic virus, yellow leaf curl virus, healthy
- Prediction with a confidence score
- Grad-CAM visual explanation
- "Low confidence, seek expert review" flag (a prototype feature, not a safety guarantee; see Limitations)
- Lab-versus-field gap with bootstrap confidence intervals, calibration analysis and accuracy-versus-coverage analysis
- Robustness checks (brightness, blur) and a field-data sweep
- Gradio web demo

## Methodology
1. **Data:** train on PlantVillage colour images of the eight tomato classes, capped at 600 training images per class and class-balanced with weighted sampling. A random held-out part of PlantVillage (up to 150 images per class) is used for lab validation and checkpoint selection. This lab score is flattering because PlantVillage contains near-duplicate shots of the same leaves. The headline result is the **field** score: all 744 PlantDoc images of the same classes (its train and test folders combined), never used for training in the main experiments.
2. **Model:** EfficientNet-B0 pretrained on ImageNet (torchvision weights), fine-tuned by transfer learning: classifier head for 3 epochs (learning rate 1e-3), then the whole network for 4 epochs (1e-4), AdamW, cosine schedule, mixed precision, seed 42, 224x224 input. The checkpoint is chosen on lab-validation loss only, never on field data. Augmentation: random resized crop, flips, rotation, colour jitter (lighting) and blur.
3. **Confidence:** softmax probabilities. Calibration uses temperature scaling in two ways: fitted on lab validation data and applied to field images (the zero-shot setting), and a 5-fold cross-fitted temperature and deferral threshold on PlantDoc, where each image is scored with parameters that never saw it. The cross-fitted version uses labelled field images for calibration only (not training) and is reported separately from the zero-shot headline. The demo's flag threshold is the 60th percentile of field-image confidences, so it answers the most confident 40%; it uses confidences, not labels, and is a prototype choice rather than a validated safety threshold.
4. **Explanation:** Grad-CAM on the last convolutional block of EfficientNet-B0 (coarse and qualitative).
5. **Evaluation:** accuracy and macro-F1 with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals, per-class recall with intervals, confusion matrix, expected calibration error, accuracy-versus-coverage curve, robustness (darker x0.5, brighter x1.6, blur sigma 4), Grad-CAM on random images and on confident mistakes (confidence above 0.9), and a field-data sweep (0, 10, 25 and 50 percent of all field images added to training, tested on a fixed held-out half of the field images).

## Results
Results come from one training run (seed 42) of the final notebook, executed in the development window on 20 October 2026.

| Evaluation | Accuracy | Macro-F1 | Calibration error (ECE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| PlantVillage held-out (lab) | 96.7% | 0.966 | not computed |
| PlantDoc (field, 744 unseen images, zero-shot) | 27.8% (95% CI 24.5% to 30.9%) | 0.230 (95% CI 0.198 to 0.260) | 0.408 uncalibrated; 0.466 after lab temperature scaling (T = 0.80) |

Lab-to-field gap: 68.9 percentage points (chance with 8 classes is 12.5%).
Confidence: mean confidence 68.6% versus accuracy 27.8% on field images, so the model is strongly overconfident. Cross-fitted field-calibrated ECE is 0.076; this is reported separately because it uses labelled field images for calibration.
Accuracy versus coverage (raw confidence, no calibration): 100% coverage 27.8%, 80% 30.1%, 60% 34.8%, 40% 39.7%, 20% 45.3%.
Cross-fitted deferral thresholds (uses labelled field images, so not zero-shot): a 70% accuracy target gave 70.2% accuracy on 6.3% of images; an 80% target gave 71.4% on 2.8%; a 90% target gave 57.1% on 0.9%. The 80% and 90% targets were not reached.
Confident mistakes (confidence above 0.9): 80 of 537 mistakes.
Robustness (field accuracy / macro-F1): original 27.8% / 0.230; darker 24.9% / 0.197; brighter 27.3% / 0.215; blur 25.0% / 0.197.
Field-data sweep (fixed held-out half of the field images, 372 images; accuracy / macro-F1): 0% added 28.2% / 0.242; 10% added 39.8% / 0.380; 25% added 40.9% / 0.402; 50% added 43.3% / 0.431. This is a separate experiment from the zero-shot result.
Per-class field recall: early blight 77.3%, late blight 55.9%, Septoria 23.2%, yellow leaf curl 21.1%, healthy 19.0%, leaf mold 9.9%, bacterial spot 3.6%, mosaic virus 1.9% (see `documentation/per_class_recall.csv` for confidence intervals).

Grad-CAM (qualitative, coarse): in a random sample of field images the heat sometimes falls on the diseased leaf area, but often on background, stems or other non-leaf regions, and some images carry watermarks or are not typical leaf photos. In the confident mistakes we inspected, the heat often sits on spotted leaf tissue, so the model attends to lesions but confuses similar spot diseases (for example bacterial spot predicted as Septoria or early blight). Heatmaps show where the model looked, not proof of why it decided. See `documentation/figures/gradcam_random.png` and `gradcam_confident_mistakes.png`.

What we found: the model that scores 96.7% on lab images scores 27.8% on field images, so the lab score badly overstates real-world performance. Its confidence is not trustworthy on field images; ranking by confidence gives only a modest gain (45.3% accuracy at 20% coverage), so the "seek expert review" flag is a prototype feature and not a safety guarantee. Adding a small amount of labelled field data helps most (28.2% to 39.8% with 74 images), but even 50% of the field images only reaches 43.3% in our single run.

Figures are in `documentation/figures/`: `lab_vs_field_gap.png`, `field_confusion_matrix.png`, `coverage_reliability.png`, `gradcam_random.png`, `gradcam_confident_mistakes.png`, `field_data_sweep.png`. ## Limitations
- PlantDoc has few images for some classes (for example mosaic virus, 54), so per-class results are noisy; see the confidence intervals.
- Only tomato and eight classes are covered; results may not transfer to other crops, regions or cameras.
- PlantDoc contains noisy images: some are not typical leaf photos (for example drawings, pressed plants, or images dominated by background, fruit or flowers) and some carry stock-photo watermarks. We did not clean or remove them, so they count against the field score.
- The model recognises visible leaf symptoms. It does not detect disease before symptoms appear.
- The lab-validation score comes from a random PlantVillage split that likely contains near-duplicate images, so it overstates lab performance.
- Results are from a single training run and seed; we did not measure run-to-run variation.
- The cross-fitted calibration uses labelled field images, so it is not a zero-shot result.
- The demo's flag threshold is set from the field-image confidence distribution (without labels) and is not a validated safety threshold.
- In the field-data sweep, PlantDoc crops from the same source photo could appear in both the training and held-out parts, which could flatter those numbers.
- Grad-CAM is a coarse, qualitative explanation.
- Field accuracy is only 27.8%, with very uneven per-class recall (for example 3.6% for bacterial spot and 1.9% for mosaic virus), so the model is not usable for real screening in its current form.
- Confidence is poorly calibrated on field images (mean confidence 68.6% versus 27.8% accuracy), and 80 mistakes were made with confidence above 0.9.

## Future scope
More crops, locally collected African field images, a mobile-friendly app, and larger field datasets: in our sweep, adding even a small amount of labelled field data improved held-out field accuracy from 28.2% to 39.8%, though more data and repeated runs are needed to confirm this.
